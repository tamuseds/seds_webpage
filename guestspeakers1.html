    Aim for the Moon

    • Flight Director for Apollo 7-17
    • Had his Aggie Ring brought to the Moon and back
    • Lead Flight Director for Apollo 12, 15, and 17
    • On-duty for Apollo 11 and 3
    • Director of Johnson Space Center and Deputy Director of Kennedy Space Center

    A Life Aimed at the Moon

    1. 1952

      Arrives at Texas A&M

      Griffin begins his studies at what was then the Agricultural and Mechanical College of Texas — the start of a path that would eventually run straight through Mission Control.

    2. 1956

      Graduates, Ring in Hand

      He graduates and receives his Aggie ring — the Class of '56 band that, more than a decade later, would travel farther from home than any ring before it.

    3. 1964

      Joins NASA in Houston

      Griffin joins NASA's human spaceflight program just as the Gemini and Apollo programs are taking shape, training for a seat on the Flight Director's console.

    4. Dec 1968

      Apollo 8

      Griffin is part of the Mission Control team for the first crewed flight to orbit the Moon — a Cold War answer, in his own words, to the shock of Sputnik a decade earlier.

    5. Nov 1969

      Apollo 12 — Lightning, and a Ring Along for the Ride

      36 seconds after liftoff, lightning strikes the Saturn V and knocks out power to the capsule. As Flight Director, Griffin's team calls for an obscure switch to be flipped — "SCE to auxiliary" — and the mission presses on toward the Moon. Griffin's own Aggie ring rides along in the capsule, making it the only one known to have reached the lunar surface.

    6. Apr 1970

      Apollo 13

      An oxygen tank ruptures en route to the Moon, turning a landing mission into a rescue. Griffin's shift helps plan the free-return trajectory that brings the crew home safely — NASA's best-known "successful failure."

    7. Dec 1972

      Apollo 17

      As Lead Flight Director for the final Apollo mission, Griffin arranges for a recording of the Aggie War Hymn to be beamed up as a wake-up call to the crew on the lunar surface — a bit of Aggie pride, 240,000 miles from Kyle Field.

    8. Today

      A Lasting Legacy

      Griffin goes on to direct Johnson Space Center and serve as deputy director of Kennedy Space Center. His ring now sits on permanent display at the Zachry Engineering Education Complex, alongside a simple question for every Aggie who walks by: where will your ring take you?
